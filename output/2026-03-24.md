# Concept of the Day
## Multimodal Fusion

Multimodal fusion refers to the process of combining information from multiple sources or modalities, such as vision, language, and motion. This is done to create a more comprehensive and accurate representation of the data. In the context of AI, multimodal fusion is used to improve the performance of models in tasks such as visual question answering, image-text retrieval, and human-robot interaction.

**Example:** For example, in a self-driving car, multimodal fusion can be used to combine visual information from cameras, linguistic information from voice commands, and motion information from sensors to make more accurate decisions about navigation and control.

**Why it matters:** Multimodal fusion is important because it allows AI models to capture a more complete and nuanced understanding of the world. By combining multiple sources of information, models can learn to recognize patterns and relationships that may not be apparent from a single modality. This can lead to significant improvements in performance and accuracy in a wide range of applications.

**In today's research:** Multimodal fusion is connected to today's papers through the use of graph-based architectures and reinforcement learning frameworks. For example, in the paper ThinkJEPA, a multimodal fusion approach is used to combine visual and linguistic information in latent world models. Similarly, in the paper UniMotion, a graph-based architecture is used to model the relationships between motion, text, and vision.
